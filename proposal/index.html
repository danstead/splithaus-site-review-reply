              What changed in this pass

              • No title. The organizer's note is the headline. Every generated title sounded like SplitHaus or a slogan, so the page opens with "Proposal · [house]" and then the note, set large. Nothing to edit or get wrong.
              • It reads as a proposal, not a pitch. it's written as "we" and "our," and SplitHaus appears only as "Made with SplitHaus" at the end.
              • Only what setup knows. SplitHaus doesn't know a group's history, so generated lines never claim it. Setup has no "we already use this order" answer for a rotation, so there's no "What stays the same" section. Anything about the past is in the organizer's own note.
              • Agreeing and joining are separate. While the organizer is proposing, the page ends with "What happens next": once we've talked it over, they'll open it up and you can join from the same link. No button, so nobody commits alone and nobody is the visible holdout. When the organizer turns joining on (a switch the organizer already has), a Join button moves to the top of the same page, with "Join the Oregon Coast House," or "Join as the Steads" after you tap your group. The proposal stays below for reference. Use the switch above each phone to see both.
              • A way to look, not a way to answer back. A quiet link under the ending, "See how else SplitHaus can work," opens the homepage demo of every sharing style. Someone who likes SplitHaus but not this plan can bring "what about a draft?" to the conversation. There's no counter-proposal feature, on purpose. It also reaches people who may want SplitHaus for a house of their own.
              • The tap prompt only promises what's real: "see your weeks" on the rotation (a real preview), "see your turn" on the draft (the real pick order), "see your weeks" on Match (how many weeks you get). The draft and Match calendars stay labelled as examples. Nothing is dimmed until you tap a name.
              • Every page shows the calendar. After a draft or a Match run, the calendar is what everyone lives in, so each page shows it: a preview for the rotation, and a labelled example for the draft and Match. Tapping your group lights up your color on all three.
              • Still proposals. No deadlines, no draft night, no years. The calendar is a labelled preview.
